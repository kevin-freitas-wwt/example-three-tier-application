# The Keeper of the Cedar Dark

Beneath the firs where fog hangs low,
Where moss grows thick and rivers slow,
A shadow moves on silent feet,
Too tall for man, too shy to meet.

The hikers whisper round the flame
Of something wild that has no name:
A footprint pressed in morning mud,
Eighteen inches, deep as blood.

He does not roar, he does not chase,
He only watches from his place,
Between the ferns, behind the pine,
A giant drawn in blurry line.

The cameras click, the pictures fail,
A smudge of fur, a muddy trail;
For every lens that tries to see,
He steps behind another tree.

Some call him myth and some call lies,
Yet something stirs where the forest sighs,
A howl that rolls through canyon stone
To tell the woods they're not alone.

So if you walk where cedars keep
Their ancient secrets, dark and deep,
Tread softly, friend, and hold your fear:
The Keeper knows that you are here.
